4. See how monthly returns are spread

Pick a date range and either the whole year or one month. Every ticker below uses the same choice, and each bar counts the months whose total return falls in a 1% range. Each chart fits its own scale by default, so a bond fund and a fast-moving stock are both readable; switch to the shared scale to compare their spread side by side. Expected ROI is one typical month: the middle value of each bar times how often that bar happened, added up. Compound ROI chains those same months together, one after another, to show what they came to in total. Dividends are included in both.

5. Find the strong and weak months

One bar per calendar month, January through December. Each bar is built only from that month: the January bar uses every January in the date range, the February bar every February, and so on. Expected ROI is what one of those months typically looked like. Compound ROI chains them together, as if you held only that month each year. Average, median, worst and best read straight off that month's returns. Volatility is how much they varied, so it is never negative and is drawn in grey. Green bars gained, red bars lost. Dividends are included. Narrow the bars to the months you care about, and switch to one shared up-and-down scale when you want to compare heights between funds.

The check box after each ticker narrows the choice in section 6, Test sitting in cash in weak months. Untick a box to leave that holding out of that section's bars; tick it again to bring it back. Only holdings ticked in section 3, Choose securities, appear here, so this filters them further. It changes nothing in the other sections, and every box is ticked again when the page reloads.

6. Test sitting in cash in weak months

Click a bar. The app looks for months that were weak for it in the chosen date range, meaning expected ROI, compound ROI, average and median were all negative. It then tries every combination of those months and starts you off with whichever did best. If holding all the time beats every combination, it picks none. Change the months by hand if you like; the search runs again when you switch ticker or move the date range. Both lines start with $10,000 at the close on the last trading day before the start month. The strategy sells at the close on the last trading day before each cash stretch, then buys back at the close on the first trading day after it. Cash earns nothing, and fees and taxes are not included.
